# Use Case Deep Dive: Product Manager at a Company

## Persona Profile

### Meet David
- **Age:** 32
- **Role:** Senior Product Manager at a Series B SaaS company
- **Company:** 150 employees, $15M ARR, B2B marketing platform
- **Experience:** 6 years in product, 2 at current company
- **Reports to:** VP of Product
- **Challenge:** Evaluating new product line expansion

### Context
David's company has achieved product-market fit with their core product. Leadership is exploring expansion into adjacent markets. David has been asked to evaluate 3-4 potential new product directions and present recommendations to the executive team.

### Work Pattern
- Strategic planning cycles quarterly
- OKRs tied to exploration and discovery
- Presentations to executive team monthly
- Cross-functional collaboration with engineering, design, marketing
- Data-driven decision making expected

### Pain Points
1. **New Territory:** "I'm great at iterating existing products, but evaluating new opportunities is different"
2. **Time Constraints:** "I have 4 weeks to evaluate 4 directions alongside my day job"
3. **Stakeholder Alignment:** "Different execs have different criteria for what makes a good expansion"
4. **Structured Framework:** "I need a consistent way to compare very different opportunities"
5. **Build vs. Buy:** "Some expansions might be better served by acquisition"

## The User Journey

### Stage 1: Discovery

**How David Finds Idea Analyzer:**

Scenario A: **Research Mode**
> David googles "product expansion evaluation framework" and finds Idea Analyzer mentioned in a product management blog post.

Scenario B: **Community Recommendation**
> In a PM Slack community (Product-Led Growth, Lenny's community), someone mentions using it for internal innovation projects.

Scenario C: **Conference Learning**
> Attends a talk on "Evaluating Product Line Extensions" that references structured idea validation.

### Stage 2: Adapting for Corporate Context

**The Challenge:**
Idea Analyzer is designed for startups. David needs to adapt it for internal corporate innovation.

**Reframing the Inputs:**

Instead of "founder context," David considers:
- Company resources and capabilities
- Existing customer relationships
- Go-to-market infrastructure
- Engineering capacity
- Brand equity to leverage

**Modified Input for Corporate Use:**

```
Product Name: AI Content Assistant (expansion product)
Category: B2B SaaS
Thesis: Our marketing platform customers struggle with content creation.
        An AI-powered content assistant, integrated with our platform,
        helps them create marketing copy faster.
Target User: Existing customers (marketing teams at mid-market companies)
MVP Features:
- AI writing suggestions in-platform
- Template library for common content types
- Brand voice training
- Performance prediction
Pricing: $200/mo add-on module
Distribution: Upsell to 2,000 existing customers + new customer hook
```

**Founder Context (adapted for company):**
```
Technical Skill: Expert (20+ person engineering team)
Domain Experience: 5 years building for marketers, deep customer
                   relationships
Existing Audience: 2,000 paying customers, 50K email list
Hours/Week: Full team available (not constrained)
Runway: 36+ months (Series B funded)
Risk Tolerance: Moderate (don't want to distract from core)
```

### Stage 3: Multi-Direction Evaluation

**David's 4 Expansion Directions:**

1. **AI Content Assistant** - Help customers create content faster
2. **Analytics Deep Dive** - Advanced attribution and insights
3. **SMB Tier** - Downmarket expansion with simpler product
4. **Agency Platform** - Tools for agencies managing multiple clients

**Evaluation Matrix:**

| Direction | Score | Strengths | Risks | Strategic Fit |
|-----------|-------|-----------|-------|---------------|
| AI Content | 7.5 | Hot market, existing customers | Crowded, fast-moving | High |
| Analytics | 6.8 | Upsell potential, differentiation | Complex build | Medium |
| SMB Tier | 5.5 | Market size, volume | Brand dilution, support costs | Low |
| Agency | 7.2 | Network effects, higher ARPU | Different buyer | High |

### Stage 4: Presentation Preparation

**What David Needs for Exec Presentation:**

1. **One-pager per direction:**
   - Opportunity summary
   - Market analysis
   - Competitive landscape
   - Resource requirements
   - Risk assessment
   - Recommendation

2. **Comparison framework:**
   - Consistent scoring across directions
   - Trade-off visualization
   - Recommendation with rationale

3. **Due diligence evidence:**
   - Not just "I think" but structured analysis
   - Defensible methodology
   - Areas for further investigation

**Idea Analyzer Output Adapted:**

For exec presentation, David transforms the analysis into:

```
PRODUCT EXPANSION EVALUATION: AI Content Assistant
==================================================

RECOMMENDATION: Proceed to Customer Discovery Phase

OPPORTUNITY SCORE: 7.5/10

STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT:
- Leverages existing customer relationships [+]
- Builds on platform integration advantage [+]
- Addresses validated customer pain point [+]
- Requires new AI/ML capabilities [?]

MARKET ASSESSMENT:
- Market Size: $10B+ content creation tools market
- Growth Rate: 25%+ CAGR
- Competition: High (Jasper, Copy.ai, Writer, etc.)
- Differentiation: Integration with our platform

RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS:
- Engineering: 4-6 person team, 6-month MVP
- Hiring: 2 ML engineers needed
- Investment: $1-2M to MVP

RISKS & MITIGATIONS:
1. Crowded market → Compete on integration, not features
2. AI quality expectations → Start with narrow use cases
3. Cannibalization → Price as add-on, not replacement

NEXT STEPS (30-Day Plan):
1. Interview 20 customers about content creation pain
2. Audit top 3 competitors (Jasper, Writer, Copy.ai)
3. Prototype integration concept with design team
4. Estimate engineering effort with tech lead
5. Build business case with finance
```

### Stage 5: Cross-Functional Alignment

**Using Analysis for Team Alignment:**

David shares Idea Analyzer output with:
- **Engineering Lead:** "Here's why we think this is worth exploring"
- **Design Lead:** "These are the user needs we should validate"
- **Marketing Lead:** "Here's the competitive landscape"
- **Finance:** "Here's the preliminary business case"

Consistent framework = easier alignment.
